# Precog commercial validation and research plan

**Prepared:** 2026-09-22
**Scope update:** 2026-10-06 — any small business of about 2 to 50 people, and the
bookkeepers and CPAs who serve many of them. Industry templates are content
packs. The September dental-group scope is superseded. Sections below follow
the updated scope. The interview scripts, experiments, and go/no-go arithmetic
are unchanged except where they named a single industry.
**Decision horizon:** 12 weeks for initial go/no-go evidence; 12 months for a
commercially credible system of record

## 1. Honest starting position

Precog is a strong **control-design and advisory product**. It is not yet a
commercial control-operations platform.

The current product can explain responsibilities, visualize authority,
identify modeled conflicts, stress-test continuity, compare assessment
snapshots, and assemble a report. Those capabilities are differentiated and
demo well. They do not yet prove that a customer's real system access matches
the model or that a selected control operated throughout the period.

Commercial viability is plausible, not proven. The thesis to test is:

> Precog helps any small business, and the firm that serves many of them,
> record who can do what, compare that record to an access or accounting
> export, collect monthly evidence, and close control gaps without buying
> enterprise GRC software. Industry packs change titles, cases, and checklists.
> They are not separate products.

The weak thesis is a low-price, self-service fraud-prevention dashboard for a
one-person shop, or a claim that Precog prevented a stated dollar loss. A very
small team has little to segregate, little time to maintain a model, and a high
support-to-fee ratio. The free plan can start that business. Paid use has to
show a second month.

### The decisive commercial question

Will a named firm pay for and repeatedly complete a monthly workflow that
keeps people, access, evidence, and remediation synchronized across clients
that are not all in the same industry?

If the answer is no, additional scoring will not create a business. If the
answer is yes only inside one pack, maintain that pack and keep the others as
templates. Do not rebrand the company as that industry, and do not claim the
horizontal product is proven.

## 2. Commercial hypotheses to test

Every hypothesis must have an owner, evidence source, deadline, and explicit
pass/fail threshold.

## 3. Target segments and buyer map

### Priority 1 — bookkeepers, CPAs, and fractional CFOs with mixed clients

**Economic buyer:** the firm owner or the partner who prices advisory work.
**Daily user:** the bookkeeper, senior associate, or fractional controller.
**Trigger:** a monthly close, a new client, a sale of the business, a suspected
loss, an insurance renewal, or a lender request.
**Job to be done:** run one review method across unlike clients, with less
spreadsheet work and a report the firm will sign as its own work.
**What they must believe:** Precog shortens the review, keeps clients
consistent, and does not replace professional judgment. Precog's name on a
report means the tool prepared it. It is not an audit.

### Priority 2 — the small business itself, about 10–50 people

**Economic buyer:** the owner, controller, or operator.
**Daily user:** the person who does the books, or the office manager.
**Trigger:** turnover, a new location, a payment loss, or an outside question
about who can release money.
**Job to be done:** know who can initiate, approve, record, hold, and reconcile
high-risk activity.
**How they arrive:** often through their accountant. The free plan is the
direct start. It is not the revenue plan.

### Priority 3 — a change of ownership or a new site

Sell a fixed-scope assessment before a broad subscription. Deliver the map, the
access inventory the customer could export, the critical conflicts, the
continuity gaps, the accepted compensating controls, and a dated remediation
list. Any industry pack.

### Defer

- A paid plan whose only user is a one-person shop. The free plan covers that start.
- Enterprises that require SSO, SCIM, a security questionnaire Precog cannot yet
  answer, and a mature assurance report before purchase.
- Insurer or lender distribution until observed results exist.
- Making one industry the company. A pack that sells can be maintained first.
  The product remains the shared engine.

## 4. Subject-matter research program

### 4.1 Internal-control model

Build an evidence table for every entitlement, conflict rule, control measure,
and fallback. Each record should contain:

- authoritative principle or source;
- which industry packs it applies to, and the small-business reading in each;
- risk or failure mode;
- directive, preventive, detective, and corrective options;
- optimal segregation and the acceptable small-team fallback;
- evidence expected;
- testing procedure, sample, frequency, and reviewer qualification;
- known limitations and circumstances requiring expert advice;
- ontology version, effective date, editor, and approver.

Research anchors to validate directly include COSO Internal Control, the 2025
GAO Green Book, NIST CSF 2.0, FBI business-email-compromise guidance, applicable
state privacy and breach rules, bank treasury practice, and payroll-provider
controls. Pack-specific guidance (for example dental, construction, or
nonprofit) is a source for that pack, not a source for every customer. Legal
or regulatory interpretations require qualified counsel. The product should
distinguish a requirement from a leading practice.

Protected health information stays out of Precog for every pack. There is no
business associate agreement. A healthcare pack does not change that rule.

### 4.2 Field validation panels

Recruit a standing editorial panel that is not from one industry:

- 2 accountants or outsourced controllers who serve mixed small businesses;
- 2 operators, from two different packs;
- 1 person who posts payments or refunds;
- 1 fraud examiner;
- 1 managed IT or identity administrator;
- 1 insurance or banking risk specialist;
- 1 owner of a small team who must use compensating controls.

A dental biller, a restaurant manager, or a job-cost bookkeeper may sit on the
panel for the pack they know. They do not define rules for packs they do not know.

Run rule review in blinded rounds. Ask each expert to rate correctness,
severity, applicability, false-positive risk, recommended evidence, and viable
fallback. Publish disagreements rather than forcing a false consensus.

### 4.3 Workflow observation

Do not rely only on interviews. Observe at least eight real workflows, across
more than one pack where the work exists:

1. taking in the day's money, closeout, and deposit;
2. posting payments and matching them to what the bank received;
3. refunds, adjustments, credits, and write-offs;
4. vendor setup, invoice entry, and ACH or check release;
5. payroll changes, processing, and release;
6. joiner, mover, and leaver access changes;
7. month-end bank and ledger reconciliation;
8. backup monitoring and recovery testing.

Capture systems touched, actors, handoffs, evidence, exception paths, elapsed
time, rework, informal workarounds, and where the documented process differs
from reality. Do not collect patient data, payment-card numbers, or government
identifiers.

## 5. Competitive research plan

The comparison must be reproducible and dated. Use official product pages,
demos, help centers, security documentation, contracts, and customer references.
Do not treat vendor marketing as verified capability.

### Categories to benchmark

1. **Systems the customer already runs:** QuickBooks and peers, Gusto, ADP, and
   the bank's positive pay or dual control. Precog has to fit beside these.
2. **Enterprise SoD and GRC:** Pathlock, SafePaaS, Fastpath, SAP and Oracle risk
   products, AuditBoard, and Workiva. These are what a buyer hears about and
   cannot afford.
3. **Substitutes:** spreadsheets, CPA workpapers, insurer questionnaires,
   managed IT reviews, access reports, and the owner's review.
4. **Industry analytics, as neighbors of a pack only:** for the dental pack,
   Dental Intelligence, Practice by Numbers, Jarvis, Denticon, and CareStack.
   Repeat the exercise for another pack only when that pack is in a paid pilot.
   Clinical AI (Overjet, Pearl) competes for budget inside a clinic. It is not
   a substitute for this product.

### Benchmark dimensions

Score only observed capability and attach evidence:

- target customer and buyer;
- implementation time and required services;
- pricing unit and minimum contract;
- accounting, payroll, bank, and identity connectors;
- actual-access ingestion and unmatched-permission handling;
- SoD policy depth and whether a small team can record a fallback;
- evidence requests, testing, conclusions, and remediation;
- certification campaigns and reminders;
- immutable audit history;
- multi-location and firm/client tenancy;
- reports, exports, and data portability;
- SSO/SCIM, tenant isolation, encryption, recovery, and SOC 2;
- whether the vendor will accept protected health information (Precog will not);
- accessibility and narrow-screen usability;
- observed-value reporting;
- contract lock-in and implementation burden.

### Mystery-shop protocol

Use one scenario for every vendor: a bookkeeping firm with three clients — a
retailer, a restaurant, and a professional office — about 8 to 25 workers each,
QuickBooks, outsourced payroll, and one bank. Ask the vendor to demonstrate
vendor setup and payment conflicts, refund conflicts, a terminated user's
access, evidence retention, reviewer sign-off, and a second client that does
not reuse the first client's job titles. A five-location single-industry group
is a later scenario, used only when a multi-site customer is in a pilot.

## 6. Customer discovery instruments

### Buyer interview — 45 minutes

1. Tell us about the last time you reviewed responsibilities or access.
2. What triggered it, who owned it, and what was delivered?
3. Show the artifact or report if permitted.
4. Which systems and exports were required?
5. Where did the process stall or require rework?
6. Which findings were rejected, and why?
7. What happens after a finding is accepted?
8. How often is the work repeated, and who ensures completion?
9. What did the last cycle cost in internal and external hours?
10. Which budget owns the work?
11. What security, procurement, and integration conditions are mandatory?
12. Ask for a paid pilot commitment at a stated price—not a satisfaction score.

Avoid asking whether the idea sounds useful. Past behavior, artifacts, budget,
and a concrete commitment are stronger evidence.

### Usability study — 90 minutes

Give participants a realistic staff roster and access export from their own
pack. Measure time and errors while they:

- build or import the team;
- resolve unmatched permissions;
- identify the three most important conflicts;
- choose a compensating control;
- assign an owner and evidence request;
- model an absence and a proposed hire;
- approve or reject a remediation;
- export a report;
- return later and explain what changed.

Record task completion, wrong turns, assistance, terminology confusion,
confidence, and whether the final conclusion matches an expert answer key.

## 7. Product experiments

### Experiment A — concierge assessment

Run five paid assessments, across at least three industry packs, with the
product supporting the service rather than hiding it. Establish baseline hours
and produce a recorded finding disposition. This tests value before expensive
integrations.

### Experiment B — CSV and QuickBooks reconciliation

Use the mapping already started for user, system, role, permission, location,
status, last-login date, and manager, plus the QuickBooks vendor and employee
read. Measure automatic mapping coverage, unmatched items, false conflicts,
reviewer time, and changes accepted.

### Experiment C — second monthly cycle

Run two complete cycles. Measure completion, reminders, delegation, reviewer
disagreement, evidence sufficiency, access that changed, and time relative to
the customer's previous method.

### Experiment D — firm portfolio

Give one firm three clients in more than one pack, reusable checks, reports,
and client isolation. Measure setup reuse, partner review time, and whether
the firm will add a fourth client.

### Experiment E — pricing

Test three concrete offers rather than abstract willingness-to-pay questions:

1. fixed-fee assessment with a defined deliverable;
2. assessment that converts to the Firm plan;
3. Firm plan priced by active client business.

Track proposal-to-paid conversion, objections, discounting, implementation
hours, and gross margin. Avoid pricing as a percentage of hypothetical loss.
Publish every Firm tier. A missing price is not an offer.

## 8. Proving value honestly

### Observed value

- hours saved in mapping, review, evidence collection, and reporting;
- external advisory hours avoided or converted to higher-value work;
- direct recoveries and duplicate or unsupported payments stopped;
- aged credits, missed deposits, payroll errors, or access exceptions resolved;
- cycle-time reduction for leavers, confirmations, and remediation.

### Control-environment movement

- percentage of critical duties with an owner and backup;
- critical conflicts eliminated, dual-controlled, or formally accepted;
- actual access matching approved access;
- evidence completion;
- median exception age;
- privileged and terminated accounts reviewed on time;
- backup restore-test pass rate.

### Modeled avoided loss

Keep modeled outcomes separate from observed value. Show low, base, and high.
Source and date every assumption. Never present an incident-free period as
proof of savings. A library case describes another organization.

### Stronger causal evidence

After enough sites exist, use stepped rollout, matched sites, or a
difference-in-differences comparison. Pre-register metrics and exclusions.
Track adverse outcomes as well as positive findings.

## 9. Product and engineering review plan

### Domain correctness

- golden test cases written and approved by a named reviewer;
- precision, recall, and disposition reason for each conflict rule;
- deterministic results independent of array order and import source;
- version migration tests for ontology, templates, controls, and snapshots;
- property tests for score bounds, planner monotonicity, and safe remediation;
- explicit tests for no-person, one-person, outsourced, and owner-override cases;
- a rule change that names the packs it applies to.

### Trust boundaries and security

- normalize the entire dual-release policy, not only its containers;
- authorize and tenant-scope every server operation;
- make quotas and writes concurrency-safe;
- use append-only audit events the application's database role cannot rewrite
  or delete, including for approvals, imports, restores, and deletion;
- add optimistic concurrency for drafts and accepted baselines;
- document data classification, retention, deletion, backup, and recovery;
- complete threat modeling, dependency scanning, secret scanning, and abuse cases;
- multi-factor sign-in for firm owners and anyone who can lock a report, share
  a link, or connect QuickBooks;
- a SOC 2 path when a buyer requires it.

Do not establish a business associate agreement. Do not store protected health
information, payment-card numbers, bank account numbers, or government
identifiers in any pack.

### Reliability and observability

- end-to-end checks for sign-in, authorization isolation, snapshot lifecycle,
  restore, import, comparison, assignment change, and evidence approval;
- structured errors and request correlation without sensitive payload logging;
- server-function latency and failure metrics;
- database migration, backup, and restore drills;
- model timeout, cost ceiling, redaction, and deterministic fallback metrics.

### Accessibility and usability

- keyboard-only navigation and visible focus;
- semantic tabs, tables, dialogs, and status announcements;
- chart and graph alternatives;
- reduced-motion behavior;
- contrast verification and 200% zoom;
- narrow viewport and large-team matrix performance;
- URL-addressable workspace and investigation state.

### Maintainability

- split the largest UI components into domain hooks and focused panels;
- centralize safe browser-storage parsing and version migration;
- keep one test runner and one scoring version on screen and in print;
- document architectural boundaries and prohibited client/server imports;
- measure bundle and render performance in CI.

## 10. Twelve-week validation schedule

### Weeks 1–2 — preparation

- recruit 20 interviewees and the editorial panel, from more than one pack;
- define the standard firm scenario (three unlike clients) and evidence templates;
- instrument baseline time and outcome measures;
- prepare priced pilot contracts and data-handling terms;
- confirm the access-import and QuickBooks paths and the finding-disposition record.

### Weeks 3–4 — discovery

- complete 12 firm interviews and 8 owner interviews;
- observe four workflows, not all in one pack;
- obtain at least five sanitized artifacts;
- issue five priced proposals;
- update a conflict rule only through a recorded editorial decision.

### Weeks 5–8 — paid design partners

- onboard five paid pilots in at least three templates;
- time every implementation step;
- independently review every critical finding;
- measure mapping coverage, false positives, and remediation acceptance;
- ship only blockers to completing the second month.

### Weeks 9–10 — second cycle

- repeat the review with the same customers;
- compare completion time, changes, and unresolved exceptions;
- test reuse across the firm's other packs;
- collect renewal or expansion decisions.

### Weeks 11–12 — decision

- analyze results against the thresholds in this plan;
- calculate gross margin including implementation and support;
- document failed hypotheses and which packs did not pay;
- decide go, maintain one pack harder, or stop;
- publish the next six-month roadmap only after the decision.

| Data             | Permissions automatically mapped                      |                 At least 80% |
| Workflow         | Second cycle completed on time                        |                 At least 60% |
| Risk             | Unresolved critical conflicts at day 90               |            Down at least 50% |

| Economics        | Implementation after packs are reused                 | Below 25% of first-year fees |

## 12. Go, narrow, or stop

### Continue toward a commercial product when

- at least three paid pilots commit to a second month;
- a firm, not only an owner, is the recurring buyer;
- findings are accurate, actionable, and faster to produce;
- one firm wants clients on more than one pack;
- customers will maintain access and evidence, not only admire the report;
- delivery can reach an acceptable gross margin without custom engineering per client.

### Maintain one pack harder when

- assessments sell but the second month does not;
- firms use the tool but clients will not touch it;
- one workflow — payment authority, or access confirmation — creates most of the value;
- customers pay for a service-assisted product but not unattended software;
- every paying customer is in one industry pack.

In those cases, build the offer that has a buyer. Keep the shared engine. Do
not describe one pack's results as proof for every small business, and do not
delete the other templates to match a single win.

### Stop treating it as a standalone venture when

- buyers praise the demo but repeatedly refuse paid pilots;
- no one owns the second review;
- finding validity remains below 50% after tuning;
- every customer requires a unique integration and a unique policy;
- implementation and support consume most of first-year revenue;
- the value story depends on hypothetical fraud savings.

The result can still be a useful internal method. That is a successful outcome
if the evidence does not support a software company.

## 13. Research-source register

Before external use, record retrieval date, document version, exact claim,
location in the source, reviewer, and the date it was superseded. Initial
sources for the shared engine:

- COSO Internal Control framework and implementation materials;
- GAO Green Book (2025 revision);
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 and its small-business resources;
- FBI business-email-compromise guidance;
- official product, security, and help-center pages for each benchmarked vendor;
- customer contracts, access exports, and workflow artifacts obtained with permission.

Pack-specific sources, including professional-association guidance, are filed
on that pack's rules. They are not applied to every customer.

Automated web access was unavailable when this plan was first drafted, so it
does not assert newly verified competitor features, prices, or market sizes.
Those remain research tasks.
