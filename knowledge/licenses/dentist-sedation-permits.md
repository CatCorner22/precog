# Dentist anesthesia and sedation permits — what Tennessee requires

**Rule.** The patient's expected depth of sedation, not the route, decides which rule applies; nitrous oxide and anxiolysis need no permit, conscious sedation needs a limited or comprehensive permit, and deep sedation/general anesthesia needs its own permit (Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0460-02-.07(2)-(3)).

## Levels
| Level | Permit | Who may give it / conditions | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrous oxide inhalation analgesia | None | Dentist, or a certified hygienist under direct supervision; the administering or supervising dentist on the premises the whole time; constant monitoring by the dentist or a hygienist/RDA certified under 0460-03-.06 or 0460-04-.05; equipment guarantees at least 30% oxygen and has a scavenger | (2)(a), (4) |
| Antianxiety premedication (anxiolysis) | None, if a single enteral drug at no more than the maximum recommended dose (MRD) for nonmonitored home use; nitrous may be added | Ordering dentist responsible; auxiliary staff certified in BLS for Healthcare Providers or CPR/AED for Professional Rescuers (in-person, manikin skills test). Exceeding the MRD requires a limited conscious sedation permit | (2)(b), (5)(a)-(b) |
| Limited conscious sedation | Limited permit | Enteral and/or combination inhalation-enteral only; ACLS (pediatric dentists may use PALS) plus an ADA-accredited postdoctoral program or a CE course of at least 24 didactic hours plus 10 clinically oriented experiences | (2)(c)1., (6)(a)1. |
| Comprehensive conscious sedation | Comprehensive permit | Enteral, inhalation-enteral or parenteral (for example, IV); ACLS (or PALS for pediatric dentists) plus an ADA-accredited postdoctoral program or a CE course of at least 60 didactic hours plus management of at least 20 patients | (2)(c)2., (6)(a)2. |
| Deep sedation / general anesthesia | Deep sedation/GA permit | ACLS (or PALS for pediatric dentists) plus 1 of: at least 1 year of advanced anesthesiology training per the ADA teaching guidelines; a CODA-approved oral and maxillofacial surgery program; or a 1-year general anesthesia residency approved for the ADSA Fellowship, or Diplomate of the American Board of Dental Anesthesiology. Pediatric deep sedation/GA needs proof of pediatric sedation, anesthesia and resuscitation training. A GA permit holder may also give conscious sedation | (2)(d), (7)(a)1., (7)(a)4. |
| Using an outside anesthesia provider | No dentist permit if the provider is a hospital anesthesiology-staff physician (MD/DO) or a dentist with a deep sedation/GA permit, who stays on the premises until every patient meets discharge criteria, and the office meets (7)(b) | A dentist who uses a CRNA must hold a deep sedation/GA permit | (7)(a)2.-3. |

## Children 13 and under
- Any conscious sedation, by any method, needs a comprehensive conscious sedation permit. (2)(c)3.(i)
- All antianxiety premedication and all sedation techniques except nitrous oxide and oxygen need a comprehensive conscious sedation permit. (5)(c)
- Sedation or anesthesia agents must be given under the dentist's direct supervision. (2)(c)3.(ii)

## Cautions
- "The degree of sedation or consciousness level of a patient is the determinant for the application of these rules, not the route of administration." (3)(a)
- Hygienists and assistants never give conscious sedation or general anesthesia (0460-03-.09(7)(h); 0460-04-.08(4)(h)).
- Deep sedation/GA offices: treatment room for an operating team of at least 3, a chair or table that allows airway management and CPR, adequate lighting, oral and pharyngeal suction, and an oxygen delivery system with full-face masks. (7)(b)1.
- Certified monitors may stop nitrous oxide and must tell the dentist of any change that might mean an adverse effect. (4)(c)
