# Get a parent's consent before treating or prescribing for a minor

**Rule.** A healthcare provider must not knowingly treat, diagnose, operate on, or prescribe or give any drug to a minor without first getting a parent's consent; "minor" excludes emancipated minors, emergencies under § 63-6-222, service members and veterans, and minors who are custodial parents.

**Exact text.**

> "a healthcare provider, or any other person shall not knowingly take any of the following actions with regard to a minor without first obtaining the consent of a parent of the minor: (1) Treat, profess to diagnose, operate on, or prescribe for any physical ailment, physical injury, or deformity; (2) Prescribe, dispense, deliver, or administer any drug or medication" (T.C.A. § 63-1-176 (Justia unofficial mirror)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Minor: under 18 (§ 63-1-176(a)(4)(A)).
- "Parent" includes a biological, legal or adoptive parent, or a person granted medical decision-making authority over the child (§ 63-1-176(a)(5)).

**Exceptions.**

- Not a "minor": emancipated under title 29, chapter 31; needs emergency treatment under § 63-6-222; current or former armed forces, reserve or national guard member; a parent with full custody of a minor child (§ 63-1-176(a)(4)(B)).

**Office checklist.**

- Confirm who signs the consent form and record that person's relationship (parent, or the person with medical decision-making authority).
- Do not seat a minor brought by a grandparent, sitter or older sibling without a parent's documented consent unless an exception applies.

**Records to keep.**

Keep the signed consent in the chart.
