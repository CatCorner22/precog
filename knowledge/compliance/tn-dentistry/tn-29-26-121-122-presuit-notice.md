# A health care liability claim starts with 60 days' written pre-suit notice

**Rule.** A claimant must give each provider who will be named written notice of a potential health care liability claim at least 60 days before suing; the notice extends the limitations and repose periods by 120 days, and a suit needing expert testimony also needs a certificate of good faith (T.C.A. §§ 29-26-121, 29-26-122).

**Exact text.**

> "shall give written notice of the potential claim to each health care provider that will be a named defendant at least sixty (60) days before the filing of a complaint based upon health care liability" (T.C.A. § 29-26-121(a)(1) (Justia unofficial mirror)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Notice at least 60 days before filing (§ 29-26-121(a)(1)).
- Limitations and repose extended 120 days for the noticed provider (§ 29-26-121(c)).

**Office checklist.**

- When a notice letter arrives, date-stamp it, send it to the owner dentist and the malpractice carrier the same day, and do not alter the chart.
- Keep a log of notices received with the date of receipt.

**Records to keep.**

Keep the notice, the envelope and the carrier report.

**Open.** The certificate of good faith requirements in § 29-26-122 and the notice contents in § 29-26-121(a)(2) are not transcribed here — check the source.
