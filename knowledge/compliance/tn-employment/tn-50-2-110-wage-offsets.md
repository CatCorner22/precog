# Offset wages for advances, loans or card charges only with a prior signed agreement and 14 days' notice

**Rule.** An employer may offset wages for a pay advance, loan or personal charges on a business card only if the employee signed a written offset agreement beforehand, the employer holds a copy, the employer gave 14 days' written notice, and the employee has not paid; a timely sworn affidavit from the employee blocks the offset.

**Exact text.**

> "The employer notifies the employee in writing fourteen (14) days prior to the payment of wages due and owing" (T.C.A. § 50-2-110(a)(3) (Justia unofficial mirror)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Signed agreement before the advance, loan or card use (§ 50-2-110(a)(2)).
- Written notice 14 days before payday, stating the amount owed, that wages may be offset, and that the employee may submit an affidavit (§ 50-2-110(a)(3)).
- The employee's sworn affidavit, with a copy to the Department of Labor and Workforce Development, no later than 7 days after the notice, blocks the offset (§ 50-2-110(b)).

**Office checklist.**

- Have every employee who gets a business card or pay advance sign the offset agreement first; file the copy.
- Use a dated 14-day notice template; a second person checks the date before payroll runs.

**Records to keep.**

Keep the signed agreement, the notice, and any affidavit.
