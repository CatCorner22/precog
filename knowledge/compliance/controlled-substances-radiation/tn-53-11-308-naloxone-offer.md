# Offer an opioid-reversal prescription when prescribing an opioid in listed situations

**Rule.** When a dentist prescribes an opioid and one or more risk conditions under the CDC opioid-prescribing guidelines are present, the dentist must offer the patient a prescription for an opioid antagonist (for example, naloxone) or another FDA-approved overdose-reversal drug.

**Exact text.**

> "when prescribing an opioid to a patient, a healthcare prescriber shall offer a prescription for an opioid antagonist, or another drug approved by the United States food and drug administration for the complete or partial reversal of an opioid overdose event, to the patient when one (1) or more of the following conditions are present in accordance with the federal centers for disease control and prevention opioid-prescribing guidelines" (T.C.A. § 53-11-308(i) (Justia unofficial mirror)).

**Office checklist.**

- Add an "opioid antagonist offered: yes / declined" line to the opioid prescription template.
- Chart the offer and the patient's answer.

**Records to keep.**

Keep the offer note in the chart.

**Triggers (as listed).** The prescriber prescribes more than a three-day supply of an opioid, and either prescribes a benzodiazepine concurrently or the patient presents with an increased risk for overdose (T.C.A. § 53-11-308(i)).
